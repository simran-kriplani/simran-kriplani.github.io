The small design decisions that shape behaviour

Field notes · 2025

Design Behaviour Systems

Behaviour is rarely shaped by one big decision. It is usually shaped by a stack of small ones: what appears first, what is made easy, what is delayed, and what is framed as the default.

We often think of influence as a matter of big ideas or memorable slogans. In practice, most of the behavioural impact comes from the architecture around the decision itself.

A well-designed interface does not require a person to become more disciplined. It reduces friction, makes the wise choice easier to notice, and respects the fact that attention is scarce and effort is costly.

That is why the most powerful interventions are often the quietest ones. They do not shout. They simply make the next step feel more obvious, more natural, and more aligned with what a person already wants.
