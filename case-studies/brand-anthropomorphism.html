Case study

Consumer Judgment & Brand Anthropomorphism Research

Research Assistant · UofT Dept. of Management

Literature synthesis GenAI-assisted research Behavioural frameworks Academic & technical writing

Consumers increasingly evaluate brands through human-like cues, especially when a brand is presented as an assistant, advisor, or agent. This raises an important strategic question: when does anthropomorphism build trust, and when does it make a brand feel inauthentic or manipulative?

My work on this project centered on synthesizing a broad literature base across consumer judgment, AI role framing, and brand anthropomorphism. By combining academic databases with generative AI research tools, I was able to review and synthesize more than 220 sources and organize the findings around how customers infer authenticity, competence, and moral intention.

The project was ultimately framed around a practical insight: people do not simply respond to brands as if they were humans; they evaluate whether the human-like cues are consistent with the brand's purpose, tone, and level of transparency. That distinction matters because the same product can feel useful in one context and manipulative in another depending on the framing.

In addition to building structure around the literature, I helped transform those insights into a 10-page manuscript and an SSHRC-style grant proposal. The goal was to bridge academic rigor with communication that would still be understandable to a non-specialist reader while preserving the depth needed for a serious research conversation.
